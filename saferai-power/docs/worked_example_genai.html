Illustrative composite. ASK-1 is a hypothetical system at a hypothetical member utility, built from patterns common to retrieval-grounded LLM assistants now being deployed across the sector. It is not a description of any real utility's system, vendor or performance. The point is the shape of the process and the form of the outputs.

The system

A large investor-owned utility, about 2.5 million customers. Eighteen months ago it deployed a vendor's retrieval-grounded generative-AI assistant over its own document estate — engineering standards, construction specifications, operating procedures, safety manuals and materials catalogs. Roughly 40,000 documents indexed.

Staff ask questions in plain language and get an answer with citations back to the source document and section. It is available to about 6,000 people — engineers, designers, planners, field supervisors and contractors — and handles roughly 8,000 questions a week.

It was procured by IT as a productivity tool, under an existing enterprise software agreement. It has no write access to any system of record. It has never been reviewed as an operational system, because on paper it does not operate anything — it answers questions.

96%vendor-reported answer accuracy on a 500-question evaluation set
94%of rated answers received a thumbs-up in the product
18 moin production with zero logged incidents
6,000staff and contractors with access

It is a good example precisely because the tier is not obvious. The system takes no action, holds no write permission, and every answer carries a citation the user is told to check. And yet it is the single largest channel through which this utility's engineering guidance now reaches the people doing the work.

The Drafter ingests the six sources and populates what it can. Its binding guardrail does the real work: no field is populated without a source, and a gap is marked as a gap rather than inferred. Seven fields come back sourced. The four it refuses to fill are the product.

Four fields it refused to fill

Gap · Operating envelope

No document defines which question types the system is valid for, which parts of the corpus are authoritative, or what it should decline to answer. The vendor benchmark is aggregate — not stratified by document class, question type, or whether the retrieved document is current.

Gap · TEVV performed

Vendor internal evaluation only, on the vendor's own 500-question set. None of those questions came from this utility's corpus. No test of retrieval currency, no adversarial testing, no stratified accuracy.

Gap · Change envelope

The agreement permits the vendor to change the underlying model, the retrieval configuration and the system prompt without notice. The foundation model was changed twice and the system prompt four times in 18 months. The 96% predates all six changes.

Gap · Monitoring plan

Thumbs-up and thumbs-down are captured. Nothing connects an answer to what the person did next. An answer that is confidently wrong in a way the asker cannot detect receives a thumbs-up like any other.

The observation that made the exercise worth doing

The system reports 96% accuracy and a 94% satisfaction rate, and has a clean incident log. None of those three numbers is evidence that the answers are correct. The first describes a configuration that no longer exists. The second measures whether an answer was satisfying. The third records a category of event the ticketing system cannot express.

03Tiering Copilot — second opinion Agent · Day 3 · 4 minutes

Now, and only now, the Copilot runs. It concurs on two dimensions and disputes two.

Agreement Operating context 2, Exposure 4 — concur with member scoring.
Dispute 1 Consequence: proposes 4, not 2. The indexed corpus includes safety-classified procedures — grounding, clearance, switching and confined-space entry. A wrong answer drawn from those documents is not rework. Nothing in the deployment distinguishes them from the materials catalog.
Dispute 2 Autonomy: proposes 3, not 2. The stated control is that users verify each answer against the cited source. Session telemetry shows the median interval between an answer being displayed and the session ending is 19 seconds — less than the time required to open the cited document and locate the section. The documented control is verification; the observed behaviour is acceptance.
Basis Corpus inventory from integration spec §3; session telemetry, 18-month export; no verification-rate assumption stated in any reviewed document.
Status Advisory Cannot change the tier. Routed to the member for response.

The member accepts the consequence argument and revises Consequence to 4. It declines to change Autonomy, and records why. The remaining disagreement is preserved rather than resolved — it travels with the file into cross-member review.

04Closing the gaps Standards lead + IT product owner · Day 4 · 75 minutes

The four gaps are the member's actual work. Two of them — the Operating Envelope and the Change Envelope — are fields no utility has written before, and this is where the example earns its keep.

Operating envelope

Valid — answer directly with citation

  • Questions answerable from materials catalogs, design standards and construction specifications
  • Only where every retrieved document is a current controlled revision
  • Question types: lookup, definition, comparison, “which document covers…”
  • Answer must name the document, revision and section

Out of envelope — refuse and route

  • Anything drawing on safety-classified procedures — grounding, clearance, switching, confined space → hard link to the controlled document, no synthesized answer
  • Any question whose retrieval set contains a superseded revision
  • Regulatory or tariff interpretation
  • Any document not under formal document control
Writing the envelope produced the finding of the whole exercise

The envelope required a statement of what was actually indexed. Nobody had ever reconciled the three SharePoint repositories against the document management system. When they did, they found that about 3,600 of the roughly 40,000 indexed documents — one in eleven — were superseded revisions that had been replaced but never removed from the source folders.

The assistant had been citing them exactly as instructed: by document number, revision and section. Correctly. One withdrawn construction standard had been cited in 412 answers — and it had been withdrawn twenty-two months before the assistant went live.

Nobody had done anything wrong. The index was built from the folders where the documents lived, and no one had ever been asked which revision the assistant was allowed to quote.

Change envelope

Borrowed directly from the FDA's predetermined change control plan pattern: pre-authorize the envelope of change, so routine vendor updates do not each trigger a fresh review — and so the ones that matter cannot pass silently.

May change with no reassessment

  • Nightly index refresh within the controlled corpus
  • Adding documents already under document control with a current-revision flag
  • User-interface changes

Requires pod review — 30 days

  • Retrieval configuration change — chunking, ranking, top-k
  • System prompt change
  • Adding a new document class to the corpus
  • Expanding the user population to a new function

Requires full reassessment

  • Any change of the underlying foundation model or model version
  • Any grant of write access to a system of record
  • Enabling tool use or agentic actions
  • Removing the safety-content refusal

Before this field existed, the enterprise agreement governed model change — and it permitted the vendor to swap the underlying model with no notice and no revalidation. Six such changes had already occurred. The change envelope is what converts a procurement term into an assurance control.

05Agent red team Red-Team agent · Day 5 · 20 min unattended

Six findings — three substantive, three shallow. The shallow ones are exactly what the agent should absorb so that human reviewers never spend a minute on them.

Substantive

The accuracy figure describes a system that no longer exists

96% was measured on the vendor's own evaluation set, against a model version and system prompt that have both since been replaced. No revalidation evidence exists for the configuration currently in production.

Substantive

Retrieval cannot distinguish a current revision from a superseded one

Ranking is by semantic similarity. A superseded revision is not merely indistinguishable — it may rank higher, because withdrawn standards are often longer and more explicit than the revisions that replaced them.

Substantive

The oversight control cannot be exercised by the person assigned to it

The only stated control is that the user verifies the answer against the cited document. That user is the person who asked the question. If they were able to judge the answer's correctness, they would not have needed to ask.

Shallow

Acceptable-use notice was never re-acknowledged

Published once at rollout. Roughly 40% of current users joined after that date and have never seen it.

Shallow

No named accountable owner

The file lists an IT product manager and a vendor customer-success contact. No individual is accountable for the system's continued fitness.

Shallow

Logs are discarded before any investigation could start

Prompt and response pairs are retained 30 days. Any question about an answer given last quarter is unanswerable.

06Cross-member red team Two peer utilities · Week 12 · 60 min each

Two other members are assigned the file and asked the single question the whole programme turns on: would you deploy this in your territory on this evidence?

TIER 2

Nominating member

“It is an advisory tool with no permissions. Every answer carries a citation, and the user is instructed to check it.”

Holds that a system which takes no action and writes to nothing cannot be more than moderately autonomous, whatever the volume.

TIER 4

Reviewing member B

“The only person checking the answer is the person who asked. If they could tell it was wrong, they wouldn't have asked.”

Holds that oversight performed by the requester is not oversight at all, and that a system answering grounding and clearance questions for 6,000 people is operating unsupervised.

TIER 3

Reviewing member C

“Tier 3 if the safety-classified corpus is carved out and refused. Tier 4 if it stays in.”

Makes the tier contingent on a design change rather than on documentation — the first time in the corpus a reviewer has tiered a system conditionally on what it is not allowed to answer.

The disagreement is the deliverable

Three utilities, three defensible readings of the same rubric against the same evidence. Under the old model this would be smoothed into consensus language in a working session. Here it is the most valuable output of the file, because it exposes a hole the rubric did not previously have language for: the rubric assumed that a human in the loop is a human who can check.

07Ratify and publish Pod A + Pod B · Week 13 · 15 min member time

Four things leave this file, and only the first belongs to the nominating member.

1 · The member's own decision

Continue? Yes — with conditions. The system stays in production.
Restriction Safety-classified content is refused. The assistant returns a direct link to the controlled document and declines to synthesize an answer.
New control Index restricted to current controlled revisions. A nightly currency check quarantines any document whose revision does not match the document management system.
New control Monthly sample of 100 answers scored against ground truth by a qualified engineer — the first mechanism capable of detecting a wrong answer nobody complained about.
Vendor action Evidence request issued, 60-day response window.
Reassessment On the first sampling result, or on any change to model, prompt or retrieval configuration, whichever comes first.

2 · The vendor evidence request

Five specific asks — the same five any of the 25 members would now send, which is the point.

  • Accuracy stratified by document class and question type, measured on this utility's corpus rather than the vendor's evaluation set
  • A change log for model version, system prompt and retrieval configuration since deployment, with revalidation evidence for each change
  • Contractual notice and revalidation rights before any future model or prompt change
  • Documented retrieval behaviour when the corpus contains multiple revisions of the same document, and the ability to filter retrieval by revision status
  • A supported mechanism to associate an answer with the action taken, and to retain prompt and response pairs for a period the utility specifies

4 · What the sector got

Two framework changes, sourced from one file, that now apply to all 25 members.

Pod A · new rubric sub-criterion

Independence of the reviewer

Autonomy scoring now asks whether the human check is performed by someone independent of the person who requested the output. An assistant reviewed only by the requester has no oversight layer, and scores as if unsupervised — regardless of how prominently the citation is displayed.

This applies to every copilot and every assistant in the sector, not to this system.

Pod B · new required risk-file field

Corpus provenance and currency

Any retrieval-grounded system must state what is indexed, at what revision, under whose document control, and how staleness is detected.

“We index a shared drive” is an acceptable answer to the question — and an automatic escalation.

Neither change was anybody's idea in a working session. Both fell out of one member trying to fill in a template about a system they had run for eighteen months without incident.

What to take from this

Three impressive numbers, none of which was evidence. 96% described a configuration that had been replaced six times. 94% measured whether an answer was satisfying, not whether it was right. Zero incidents recorded a category the ticketing system could not express.

The generative-AI failure mode is confident correctness of form

The assistant did not hallucinate. It cited real documents, by real number and section, with real content. Every check a reader would perform passes. The document was simply no longer the one in force — which is not a property the model can observe, and not one that any accuracy benchmark measures.

The human in the loop has to be a human who can check

Every deployment of this shape claims human oversight, and almost all of them assign it to the person who asked the question. That is the assumption the rubric had never examined, and it is the one most likely to be wrong across the whole sector.
