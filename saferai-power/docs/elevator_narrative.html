01 · The premise

Why this story and not a description of the program

Nobody remembers a framework. They remember that a number everyone trusted turned out to mean nothing, and that nobody had done anything wrong.

The story works because it has a reversal and no villain. A reversal makes it stick; the absence of a villain is what makes a utility executive lean in rather than get defensive — because they are hearing about their own systems, and they know it by the second sentence.

It is deliberately a generative-AI story. Assistants and copilots over internal documents are what the industry is actually nervous about right now, nearly every utility has one in flight, and it is precisely the class of system US banking regulators looked at in April and declined to govern. You will not have to explain why it matters.

Three numbers carry the whole thing. Do not add a fourth. Every extra number you introduce costs you one of these.

94%The reassuring number. Users who rated the answers helpful, over eighteen months, with no incidents.
1 in 11The turn. Indexed documents that had already been superseded — and were still being quoted, correctly, by number and section.
3.5 hrsThe relief. What it costs one person to find that.
The one line that does the most work

“Nobody did anything wrong.” Without it the story is an exposé and your listener gets defensive on behalf of their own operation. With it, the story is about a systemic blind spot they share — and they start telling you about their version of it. That reaction is the entire goal.

02 · Under twenty seconds

For “so what do you work on?”

We're getting twenty-five of them to agree on how you prove one is safe enough to trust — and what evidence to demand from the vendor.

Six thousand engineers, an AI assistant over the company's own standards. Ask a question, get an answer with a citation.

Comfortthe numbers

Eighteen months. Ninety-four percent rate it helpful. No incidents.

Turnthe reversal

Then someone checked the index. One in eleven documents it could quote had already been superseded — and it was citing them perfectly. Right number, right section. Wrong revision.

Kickerthe real point

That ninety-four percent measured whether answers were satisfying. Not whether they were right.

Landwhat we are

SAFERai.power is twenty-five utilities agreeing on how you check. Three and a half hours per system.

The mechanics of why it lands

The first line is something they are probably doing themselves this quarter. The second lulls. The third is the whole speech — “right number, right section, wrong revision” is a failure every engineer in the industry understands instantly, and no accuracy benchmark measures it.

Then the kicker does the real damage: the satisfaction score measured whether the answer was satisfying. Everyone in the room has a dashboard with a number like that on it.

05 · Three minutes

The meeting opener

Use this to open a briefing, or when someone has genuinely made room. It adds the two things the shorter versions cut: what the utility actually did about it, and why this has to be a consortium rather than a project.

3:00 The full arc 389 words

Frame

When people say AI and the grid, they mean data centres. I work on the other one — the AI inside the utility. And the part everybody is nervous about is generative: assistants, copilots, chat over our own documents. Every utility is deploying them, and almost none of them went through an operational review, because on paper they don't operate anything.

Case

Here's the shape of it. A utility puts an AI assistant over forty thousand of its own documents — engineering standards, construction specs, operating procedures, safety manuals. Six thousand engineers, designers and field supervisors can ask it anything in plain language. It answers with a citation to document, revision and section. Eighteen months in production. Ninety-six percent accurate on the vendor's benchmark. Ninety-four percent of users rate the answers helpful. Zero incidents.

Methodhow it runs

Running that through what we're building costs about three and a half hours of the utility's own people. Software pre-fills what it can from documents they already have, and more usefully, flags what's missing.

Turn 1the corpus

First thing it couldn't fill in: which documents the assistant is actually allowed to quote. Nobody had ever written that down. So they checked — and one in eleven indexed documents was a superseded revision. Replaced years ago, never deleted from the folder the index was built from. One withdrawn construction standard had been cited in four hundred and twelve answers. It was withdrawn two years before the assistant launched.

Turn 2the oversight

Second: the only control was that users verify each answer against the source document. But the person doing that verifying is the person who asked the question. If they could judge the answer, they wouldn't have needed it. Median time from answer displayed to session closed was nineteen seconds.

Absolve

Nobody did anything wrong. The index was built from the folders where the documents lived. No one had ever been asked which revision was allowed to be quoted.

Resolvewhat changed

They didn't switch it off. They restricted the index to current controlled revisions with a nightly currency check, made it refuse safety procedures and hand back the controlled document instead, started sampling a hundred answers a month against ground truth, and sent the vendor five evidence requests.

Scalewhy twenty-five

And that's why it's twenty-five utilities and not one. Those five requests are now the same five every member sends. That moves the market.

06 · Delivery

How to say it

Do

Stop after “no incidents”

A full beat. You are setting a trap, and the pause is what makes the listener commit to the comfortable reading before you turn it over.

Do

Slow down on “nobody did anything wrong”

Half your normal pace. It is the line that converts a listener from defensive to curious, and it only works if it sounds deliberate rather than thrown away.

Do

Say “wrong revision,” not “hallucination”

It did not make anything up — that is what makes it frightening. Real document, real section, real content, superseded two years ago. Every check a reader would perform passes. Engineers feel that one in their stomach.

Do

End on a noun, not a mission

“Evidence from a vendor.” “Three and a half hours.” “The same five requests.” Concrete endings invite questions; aspirational ones invite nodding.

Don't

Use a single acronym

No SOTIF, no ODD, no UL 4600, no TEVV, no tiering rubric. Every one of them is a request that your listener stop following the story and start decoding. The whole point is that the story carries the concepts without naming them.

Don't

Open with what the program is

“SAFERai.power is an EPRI-led collaborative that will develop a risk framework…” loses the room in nine words. The program is the last thing you say, once they want to know who's fixing it.

Don't

Add a fourth number

Ninety-four, one-in-eleven, three and a half. Forty thousand documents and six thousand users are fine as colour in the longer versions; adding 96%, 412 citations, 25 members and 2,000 hours all at once means they remember none of them.

Don't

Claim it's a real utility

It's a composite built from patterns common to retrieval-grounded assistants now being rolled out across the sector. Say “here's the shape of it” or “a system like this.” If someone asks directly, answer directly — in a room full of people deploying exactly this, a fudge is expensive and the story works just as well as an archetype.

07 · Interrupts

When they cut in — which means it worked

Getting interrupted is success. Have these ready at about fifteen seconds each, then stop and let them steer.

They ask

“Isn't that just a data-quality problem?”

That's the comfortable reading, and it's half right. But the model was working perfectly — it retrieved a real document and quoted it accurately. Nothing in the system can tell a current revision from a withdrawn one, and nothing in an accuracy benchmark would ever catch it. Fix the folder and you've fixed one instance, not the class.

They ask

“How's this different from the data centre stuff?”

Completely different problem. That's AI as a load on the grid — FERC and NERC are already on it. This is AI as a tool inside the utility, making operational decisions. Same two words, nothing else in common.

They ask

“What would you need from me?”

Two real systems you already have running, and about eighty hours of one good person's time. Not a committee seat — two actual systems, with their actual gaps. You get the assessment on your own systems either way.

They ask

“Who else is in it?”

About twenty-eight organisations. Most of the big investor-owned utilities, public power, the market operators — and NERC. Plus the technology vendors, which matters, because the point is agreeing what evidence they have to hand over.

They ask

“We're about to launch one of these. What do I do Monday?”

Three things, and none of them need us. Write down which documents it's allowed to quote and at what revision. Find out whether anyone other than the person asking ever checks an answer. And get a contract term that stops your vendor swapping the underlying model without telling you.

They ask

“Doesn't this slow everything down?”

It's the opposite problem. Right now people either deploy with no basis or stall for months arguing. Three and a half hours to a documented decision is faster than the argument you're having today — and it's defensible afterwards.

They ask

“So what happened to that system?”

Still running. They limited it to current controlled revisions with a nightly check, made it refuse safety procedures and hand back the controlled document instead, and started scoring a hundred answers a month against ground truth — the first mechanism that could catch a wrong answer nobody complained about.

08 · Variants

Same story, different last line

Never rewrite the narrative for the audience. Change only the closing sentence — the story is doing the persuading, and the last line just points it at what they own.

CEO / board

This is how you say yes to AI faster, and be able to explain the yes afterwards.

CISO / risk

You get a documented boundary and a monitoring trigger, instead of a vendor's aggregate number.

Operations / engineering

Your people keep the assistant. The difference is it can only quote the revision that's actually in force.

Vendor / technology member

Build the evidence package once, and it satisfies twenty-five buyers instead of twenty-five questionnaires.

Regulator / NERC

The sector is documenting its own failure modes before anybody has to ask it to.

Prospective member

Eighty hours of one person's time — and you take the whole method home and run it on your own portfolio.

If you only remember one thing

Open with the assistant, pause after “no incidents,” turn on “one in eleven had already been superseded,” land the kicker — “it measured whether the answer was satisfying, not whether it was right” — and slow down for “nobody did anything wrong.” Everything else is expansion.
